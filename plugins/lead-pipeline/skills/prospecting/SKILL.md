Start with leads_offers (the pitch: what it is and who it is for) and leads_known (who is already in, for every offer).

Where to look, in this order:
- Who has the problem now: news of launches, funding, hiring for the role the offer helps, public roadmaps, conference talks, job posts, changelogs.
- Who already buys something similar: customer pages and case studies of comparable products, marketplaces, directories.
- Communities where the need is discussed, to find organisations (not individuals) behind it.

A fit has three parts, all from the organisation's own pages or reputable reporting: a need the pitch answers, a reason it matters now, and the ability to buy (a business, active, of a size the offer suits). Check each candidate on its own site before adding it.

Record with leads_add, one per organisation:
- why: two lines, the need and the "now", in your words, with the evidence on the source page.
- channel and contact: a published business route only: a role address on their site (hello@, sales@, partnerships@), a company LinkedIn page, a contact form URL. If none is published, channel other and contact empty.
- source: the page with the evidence.

Never collect: personal email addresses, private phone numbers, home addresses, or anything about a person beyond their public business role. Under data-protection law the operator must be able to explain why each record exists; the why line is that explanation.

Stop at about ten good prospects per run, or fewer if the evidence is thin; say what you ruled out.
